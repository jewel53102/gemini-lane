Recommended: the middle tier — county-level criminal search is the part that actually matters, and a package that only offers a "national database" search is not a defensible check on its own. Budget roughly $60–$120 per hire including verifications.

How to Conduct a Background Check

  1. Interview the candidate first. Near the end of a promising interview, mention the role is subject to a background check before start.
  2. Make the offer conditional, in writing, on passing the background check.
  3. Send the disclosure form and get written authorization back — two separate documents, not combined with the offer letter or anything else.
  4. Order the check the same day authorization comes back. While it runs, make reference calls in parallel — don't wait on the report first.
  5. If the report is clean: confirm the start date and file the signed disclosure, authorization, and report in a restricted personnel folder (never the client-facing Drive structure).
  6. If something surfaces: don't auto-disqualify. Weigh the nature of it, how long ago it was, and its relevance to the job. If it might change the decision, send the candidate the report plus a pre-adverse notice, wait five business days, then send a final adverse-action notice if the decision stands.
