Post-Consult Onboarding Automation
Monday + Mailchimp + Zapier + Calendly + PandaDoc. Captured from Sarah walking the Monday board live and describing what she currently does by hand after a consult call. Anything she flagged as undecided is called out as an Open Item below rather than built in as a rule.
Two Ways a Lead Enters
Web → Books via Calendly→ Consult Booked (Calendly)
Lead Calls In→ Sarah manually books the consult in Calendly→ Consult Booked (Calendly)
Everything below this point assumes a Calendly booking exists, regardless of which channel got the lead there. Phone leads currently require Sarah to manually get the caller onto Calendly — she's noted this channel is growing and is harder to track than web leads. Automating that hand-off (the Quo call-menu + auto-text idea) is a separate, lower-priority research item — see the Open Items Register, Section 8 — not yet part of this build.
Pipeline at a Glance
Consult Booked (Calendly)→ Consult Held→ Email 1: Post-Consult Follow-Up→ Nudge Day 3→ Nudge Day 5–7→ Long-Term Nurture
Runs in parallel off the same trigger: once the service agreement is signed, Email 2: Welcome fires and the nudge/nurture branch stops for that lead. Calendly already owns booking confirmations and the 1-hour-before reminder (text + email) for both the consult and the intake session — this workflow doesn't rebuild those.
1Fix the Data Model First
Everything downstream depends on the record clearly identifying who receives services versus who booked the call — today the two get conflated and Sarah has to manually recode Adult Lead/Minor Lead after the fact.
Separate "client" from "contact" on the booking form

Whichever form the client fills out to book (Calendly today, possibly Monday later — see the note below), relabel the name fields: Person receiving services (first/last name), and only if different, Person reaching out (first/last name) plus Relationship to client. This is the fix for the current bug where a parent's name (e.g. "Heather Hamilton") gets used as the client name instead of the child's.

Move the consult intake form into Monday

Sarah's JotForm — labeled "Parent Consult Form" on the 2026 leads tab but actually used for every consult call, adult or parent — becomes a native Monday form tied to the lead record, so what she asks live on the call writes straight into the record instead of living in a separate tool she has to cross-reference.

Form placement is flexible — one hard requirement

Sarah has no preference whether the booking/intake form lives in Calendly or Monday. The only requirements: it captures everything the automation needs, and it's required before the booking is confirmed — if it comes after scheduling, clients skip it.

2Email 1 — Post-Consult Follow-Up
Sent right after the consult call. (Exact trigger event is still open — most likely a "Consult Held" status set on the record, since the call itself isn't something Monday or Mailchimp can observe on its own.) This is the most customized email in the sequence — three things vary independently inside it.
Always included

Personalized opening — by the client's own name if the lead is the client (adult), or "your son/daughter, [child's first name]" if it's a parent booking for a minor.

Summary of services, written directly into the email body (Sarah currently attaches this as a PDF export of her rates/services page — Mailchimp lets this live in the email itself instead).

Note that the service agreement is coming next, ideally with a "review & sign" link to PandaDoc embedded in this same email — see Section 3.

Variant 1 — which recurring-time case applies
CaseWhat happened on the callWhat the email says
A — Time agreedSarah offered specific times and the lead accepted informally on the call."You've got [time] — it's yours once the service agreement is signed." (Not held/blocked until then — this is the intentional nudge to sign.)
B — Needs to checkLead couldn't commit yet (e.g. needs to check a co-parent's or kid's schedule)."Here are some proposed times — let me know which works. I'll block it once the agreement's signed."
C — No schedule yetLead has no idea of availability yet (e.g. booking in August before the school year starts)."Send me your (or your child's) schedule and I'll send proposed times once I have it."
All three end on the same line: the time isn't confirmed until the service agreement is signed.
Variant 2 — intake session scheduling

The intake is a separate 90-minute session, distinct from the consult call.

Minor/student client: the parent (person reaching out) books it via the Calendly link included in the email.

Adult client: the adult books it themselves via the same link.

College-age judgment call: Sarah decides live on the call whether the student books alone or both parent and student each book their own intake session — not automatable, but if both are needed the email must carry the link/instruction for both.

Override: if Sarah already booked the intake live during the consult call (rush cases), suppress the "please schedule your intake" instruction entirely — driven by the All Scheduled checkbox from Section 1, checked in the same form she's already filling out so she doesn't have to remember to update anything afterward.

3Service Agreement (PandaDoc)

One base template covers both the parent-of-a-minor and adult-self-pay cases — the only difference is whether the "child's name" field is filled in, so this doesn't need two documents (the same pattern medical intake forms use for under-18 signers).

Exception — Parent Paying: when an adult client's parent or another third party is paying, two agreements go out: one to the payer (consent to be charged) and one to the client (acknowledging terms, cancellation policy, etc.). Triggered by the Parent Paying checkbox from Section 1.

Open item — confirm with Sarah's attorney

Sarah isn't sure the two-agreement version is actually required. Build the branch so it's ready, but treat the requirement itself as provisional until she's confirmed with her lawyer.

Longer-term want, not yet scoped: auto-generate the PandaDoc agreement straight from the consult-intake form fields (Section 1), instead of Sarah manually retyping into a PDF and re-uploading. Depends on the Monday-hosted intake form shipping first.
4Email 2 — Welcome / Signed Confirmation

Trigger: once the service agreement is fully signed in PandaDoc.

Content: celebratory, congratulatory tone ("Welcome to OptiMind! So excited to be working with you...") that reconfirms the agreed recurring session times and the already-scheduled intake time. No separate intake reminder needed — Calendly's own reminder already covers that.

Trigger mechanism: connect Mailchimp directly to PandaDoc — that's the platform Sarah actually sends contracts from, so it's the source of truth for "signed," not Monday. Wire a Zapier automation for PandaDoc's document-completed event that fires the Mailchimp send directly, the same pattern as the Monday↔Mailchimp connector from Sprint 03/04 (Sprint 04).

Open item — confirm Zapier supports this PandaDoc trigger

Need to verify Zapier's PandaDoc integration exposes a "document completed by all signers" trigger (not just "viewed" or "completed by one party" — Sarah gets a separate Gmail notification for each of those three, so the trigger has to be the right one or Email 2 fires too early). Fallback if it doesn't: the manual Contract Confirmed checkbox from Section 1, which Sarah ticks herself to fire the email.

5If the Agreement Isn't Signed — Nudge Sequence
WhenContent
Day 3 after the consult dateWarm, low-pressure check-in — "just wanted to see if you had any questions about the service agreement" — re-share the signing link, and if intake isn't booked yet, re-share the intake scheduling link too.
Day 5–7 after the consult dateSame intent as Day 3, reworded so it doesn't read as a copy-paste repeat.

Tone rule (applies to every email in this whole workflow): "warm and professional" — personal, not corporate. This is an explicit instruction Sarah wants passed to whatever's drafting the copy, in keeping with OptiMind's brand voice.

Weekend blackout rule (applies to every automated send in this whole workflow): never send on Saturday or Sunday. If a calculated send date lands on a weekend, hold and send the next business day. Reason: she doesn't want to train clients to expect weekend responses.

Beyond ~Day 7, stop agreement nudges — move into long-term nurture (Section 6).
6Long-Term Nurture — Not Yet, Not Lost
~4 weeks after the last touch

A personal check-in, not a sales nudge — "how's [child] doing?" / "how are things going?" Most of these leads went quiet for life reasons (a death in the family, a rough semester, "we wanted to try on our own first"), not because they said no.

If they re-engage → route back into the Email 1 flow (or straight to the agreement link, whichever fits). If they stay quiet → move into the blog/content nurture bucket (future workstream, not yet built) so they keep hearing from OptiMind without a repeated direct pitch.

~3 months after that

A further check-in on the same cadence, for leads still sitting in the nurture bucket.

Exception — school-age / college-age leads

Instead of the generic 4-week/3-month clock, check in at the natural academic checkpoint — mid-January, after fall grades are in ("how did last semester / the first half of the year go?"). Suppress this one if Sarah already emailed the lead for any other reason between roughly early December and mid-January, so it doesn't double up.

"Interested Later" leads

On or near the date the lead gave (the field from Section 1), send a simple re-engagement email — "circling back, you mentioned this might be a better time — want to schedule another consult, or go straight to the service agreement?" Keep this one templated and simple; Sarah flagged that a fully personalized version would be too custom to automate.

7Graduated-Client Re-Engagement

For clients who completed services within roughly the past year, a check-in around the same academic-checkpoint timing as Section 6 — "how are things going now that you're on your own?"

Positive response → ask for a review/testimonial. Response that services are needed again → offer to restart, re-entering them into the consult flow.
